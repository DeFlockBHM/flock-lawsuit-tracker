**New lawsuit identified: Grimmett v. City of Wichita (Kansas)**

A new state constitutional challenge to Flock ALPR surveillance was filed on July 30, 2026 in Sedgwick County District Court, Kansas. 
Mason Grimmett filed a lawsuit against Wichita on July 30, represented by the Kansas Justice Institute, claiming about 200 Flock Safety cameras around the city photograph and catalog every passing vehicle without a warrant, probable cause, or judicial oversight.
 
The suit argues that program violates the Kansas Constitution's privacy protections.


The suit targets the city (deploying agency) rather than Flock Inc., though Flock is referenced. 
The complaint notes that due to the ubiquity of Wichita's Flock ALPR network, Grimmett cannot go about routine daily activities without being tracked and cataloged by the Wichita Police Department and Flock Safety, Inc.
 Notably, the case draws on documented officer misuse in Kansas: 
a former Kechi Police lieutenant used Wichita's Flock ALPRs to track his estranged wife and was found guilty in Sedgwick County District Court of unlawful use of computers and stalking.


Relief sought goes beyond damages: 
Grimmett wants the court to declare Wichita's program unconstitutional under Section 15 of the Kansas Bill of Rights and order the cameras removed.
